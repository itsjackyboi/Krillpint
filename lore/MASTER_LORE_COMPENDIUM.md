# **I. WORLD GEOGRAPHY**

The Pintland Isles occupy a temperate archipelago surrounded by wild and storied seas. The main continent is varied in terrain, jagged coastal cliffs, dense inland forests, open farming valleys, and mysterious highland ruins. Much of the western side remains largely unknown and poorly mapped.

  

  

## **Pintland Isles — Mainland**

### **Mountain Ranges**

  - Breakback Mountains — The great central mountain range. A natural barrier between the settled East and the untamed Western plains, also known as West Pint. Historic Lagendale stretched all through modern day West Pint and even further into the Wildlands, but it could never fully colonize the east partly due to these mountains and their religious superstitions about what lay beyond them.
  - Rum Ridge — A lone peak which separates Shanty Town from the Hoegaarden Plains. Previously, this was the site of a vast arena for public gladiatorial games, although the arena was subsequently torn down with plans to rebuild elsewhere.
  - Killdeath Peak — This is one of the largest hills in Pintland. Just south of Sackbeards Tavern, this hill is the home of Fentmaxxers now abandoned observatory and the place of death for beloved Aleforge mayor, Thomas Thatcher.

  

### **Forests**

  - Brandywine Brush — The dense inland forest known locally as 'Thiccy Forest.' This is the home of the Veilwalker encampment, Fenwick. Many other notable locations are shrouded by the dense forest, most prominent of these is the Thorny Thicket, a bar owned by George Bush. A disgruntled farmer has taken up residence deep in the forest as well and one of the very first shrines ever discovered by the Liquor Kings was in this forest, found by Guinnie.

  

### **Lakes & Waters**

  - The Whiskey Shallows — The large central lake. It is fed by springs originating in the Breakback Mountains which flow through the town of Name. The lake is completely surrounded on its North and Eastern side by the Brandywine Brush. To its direct West is the mountain range. Flowing further South is a small river which drains to the sea. Some scholars believe the remains of the Rotted Soul may rest here, though until recently this was unknown. The Kings discovered the true location underneath the Hoegaarden Library.
  - The Basin at Bully Hill — The lake adjacent to Providence.
  - Gulf of Aleforge — The bay in front of Sackbeard's Tavern, a natural harbor shaped by centuries of pirate traffic. This is nearby to the waters where Sackbeard killed the Great Beast.
  - Bottomless Bay — The bay in front of Hoegaarden Library. Another entry way into Providence, notably this bay is overlooked by Blodello Bridge which crosses the bay leading from Shanty Town to Providence.
  - Dead Man Hallows — The stretch of ocean littered with shipwrecks, located in the Gae Strait between the mainland and Vodkonia. 

### **Towns & Settlements**

  - Shanty Town — The pirate cliff settlement adjacent to the Tavern. A lawless place build on reputation and grog.
  - Aleforge — Brewing capital of the Isles, home of the Drunken Trials. Known for it's fine ale and people.
  - Providence — The ordered, tonic-driven holy city on the cliffs. A strict theocracy ruled by Cardinal Addy, head of the Church of Patmos.
  - Fenwick — The Veilwalker wetland settlement. Named by outsiders: 'Fen' for the marshland, 'Wick' for dwelling place. Seen as a temporary camp by the Veilwalkers, although it predates all human towns in the neighboring areas.
  - Name — A settlement on the mainland, site of former pirate crewmembers' second lives..
  - Roto Kaiishi — The famous floating merchant city anchored to the ocean floor. The only laws here are those of the free market.

### **Points of Interest**

  - Sodomire Gaol — The regional maximum security prison. This prison is for the worst of the worst and they are never leaving, most of the cells are welded shut.
  - New Haven — The aetherial realm accessible only to a select few through the Cloister.
  - Hoegaarden — An ancient library, houses the largest collection of knowledge in all of Pintland.
  - Orchenk Manufactory (The Factory) — The abandoned industrial facility where the Nethergate disaster occurred.
  - The Vanishing House (The Mansion) — Known in folklore as such; canonical name unknown.
  - Sackbeard's Tavern — The great Shell Tavern built around the carcass of the slain Tavern Beast. Each year the pirates put their differences aside and celebrate together here.
  - The Cloister — The ancient monk's hall built over a mystical anomaly. Recently it has been closed off by the Fayte Druids
  - Fentmaxxer's Observatory — The hastily constructed observatory on Killdeath Peak.
  - The Dread Marrows — A region of dread and mystery.
  - John Cum's Cumstead — The great wheat farm, now in the hands of John Rump.
  - The Shrines — A scatter of ancient shrines across the Isles, origin debated. However, at some point in their history they were used as a stash of great armor.
  - Blodello Bridge — A notable bridge landmark crossing the Bottomless Bay.

### **Regions Beyond**

  - Vodkonia — An unexplored region, currently the place of Gideon Drake's banishment.
  - West Pint and the Hoegaarden Plains — The historic heartland of the ancient Lagendale Empire. Most of West Pint is currently unmapped and not under the influence of regional powers.
  - The Great Southern Desert — Believed to mark the site where the Rotted Soul was slain. Was once fertile grassland.

# **II. LOCATIONS & MAJOR LORE**

## **Sackbeard's Tavern (The Shell Tavern)**

#### ***Lore:***

A few hundred years ago, the beast now enshrined by the tavern awoke suddenly and began attacking traders and pirates alike. This went on for months, leading to a near total shutdown of trade in the region. While this hurt the traders, it also hurt the pirates, because who are you supposed to steal from when the traders go home? This fact firmly in mind, the great pirate Sackbeard assembled an armada of the best pirate crews from around the world and laid out a plan to kill the beast. The weeks following gave rise to many heroic tales and ultimately led to the beast's demise. To honor this great feat, every crew in the region (pirate or not) gave tribute to help drag the beast's shell up to land and construct a tavern in its hollow. Once every year since that day, the many crews of the region lay down arms and come for a rowdy night of drinking, games, and song.

The tale of the tavern beast is largely related above. However, scholars still debate what exactly caused the tavern beast to start attacking ships. Local academics have drawn parallels to similar activity with the ancient beasts of the island, but as of yet there have been no clear conclusions drawn. Could the bones littered across the land all be somehow related? Do they bring ill tidings or mere benign mystery? Only time will tell.

## **Roto Kaiishi**

#### ***In Game Use:***

Because the hope is that we can all help finish Roto, it has the possibility of serving as the server's "starter village" where we will all have our own shack to hold any goods not worth storing in New Haven. As the server's survival infrastructure is developed, Roto could definitely become a trading or travel hub, but this will depend on the speed and trajectory of its development.

#### ***Lore:***

While the yearly pirate feast at the tavern began as a well kept secret, such a large gathering was bound to be discovered. It just so happened that the first one to catch on was a Chinese maritime trader named Xi Jin Ping who was at the end of a long train of very unlucky happenings. When he discovered a fleet of over 50 pirate ships, he realized he may have just found his last chance at making a decent living for himself. Thus began the first ocean-bound shop. Xi marketed his shop as being the perfect fence for plundered goods, as no one nation had legal control over the ocean. This was a HUGE success and Xi quickly turned a profit. The next step was to anchor his base of operations to the ocean floor and begin building extensions. These extensions were humble, but each one was rented out to similarly opportunistic traders until their collection became enough to be considered a marketplace. It was around this time that Roto began to become its own center of gravity- Rather than simply orbiting the pirate gatherings, Roto was becoming more and more a haven for traders of all kinds who didn't want to deal with the regulation that came alongside trading at a national port. While Xi has long since been dead, the city has continued to grow at an incredible pace, leading to the formation of local culture, laws, and even social stratification.

As mentioned above, Roto has developed into something resembling a seabourne nation-state. However, because of the strong legacy of its founder Xi, it bears a cultural identity that cannot be defined in simple government-subject terms. First, Xi was strongly opposed to the city developing too much bureaucracy. Any "law" to be implemented had to have a clear economic benefit to be considered as one of the biggest draws of Roto was its lack of regulation. One example of such a rule is a yearly set of fees for all merchants in Roto. Xi was strongly opposed to the idea of taxing any sales in Roto, but still wanting to turn a profit, he implemented a flat yearly fee that must be paid to do business in Roto. You might expect Roto to need a good security force in order to help prevent theft and keep businesses close, but they were actually able to do without this for decades because of how protective the pirates were of their one reliable fence. After Xi's death, however, Roto's elite requested more dedicated security forces; a request that was eventually met. You might also be wondering- If there aren't any real laws in Roto, is anything illegal? The answer is yes and no. While Xi held fast to the principle that all rules in Roto should be fiscally motivated, it turned out that some trades (slave trade, drug trade, dark magic markets) brought the wrong crowd and scared off potential high profile customers. For this reason, although nothing is illegal, there is a rigid system of taboos in Roto that largely determines what can be sold in the main city versus what must be sold in the underground market or after dark. Finally, we must answer the question of who controls Roto now that Xi is long dead. Many think it's a conglomerate from the Spirit realm, as that's where all the richest merchants live. However, Xi strongly believed that if the biggest players were allowed to control the market economy, they would eventually instantiate laws that would decrease natural market competition and gut Roto from the bottom up. To prevent this, he began training one of his favorite young merchants (Kim Jong Un) to eventually take his place. Once Kim had proven his dedication to Xi's principles, he took joint control of Roto and was instructed to begin training a new disciple for when Xi died. This system is very personal and one poorly chosen candidate could be the end of Roto. However, Xi believed strongly that such a system was an important counterweight to the often corrupting effects of market domination.

## **Fenwick & the Veilwalkers**

The Veilwalkers- Those who move between the seen and unseen, tied to nature spirits.

The Veilwalkers are a mystical/slightly magical nomadic tribe. They are an ancient people who date back to the beginning of man. They live up to 10 times longer than humans. Although they appear humanoid they are anything but.

At the birth of man a tribe of barbarians pillaged across the earth destroying all semblance of life and nature. The woodland spirits (nymphs, gnomes, fairies and such) could do little against them. So they used their combined magic to birth "protectors" from the earth. The protectors are what's known as the "veilwalkers". They were born from the dirt and soil of the earth. They refer to humans as "The flesh born" and themselves as "earth born". They live simple lives and live off of only what the earth provides for them. Hence they're architecture being cobbled together. They believe in a great respect for nature and the earth. They believe that nature should not conquer man nor that man should conquer nature but that they live in harmony.

They have lived on this island long before anyone else. But they keep to themselves and have no issue with people building and settling near them. They are more or less unbothered by their island neighbors. So long as the fellow islanders give due respect to the earth. Those who dare offend them or try to take too much from the earth are haunted by this tribe and are cursed by famine and disease.

When they communicate with the woodland spirits they transform into ghostly figures and dwell in the woods. People rarely see this but those who do tell the tale and have hence been named by the islanders as "VeilWalkers" due to the fact that they look like they have a translucent veil laid over them when they are in their spirit form. They are also referred to as "healers", "mystics", "gardeners" and even "the dumb monks in the cave"

They are nomadic but only relative to their own life expectancy. So relative to humans they never leave whatever place they've settled in. It'll be hundreds of years before they move again. They travel across the world going wherever the winds and the waters take them. As they believe it, it is wherever the earth needs them.

They have no name for themselves, they simply have no need for them given their mystical nature. Which is something the "flesh born" are uncomfortable with and can't really understand. So naturally wherever they travel they are given new names by the locals. These locals call them "veilwalkers" but they have gone by many different names throughout history. The locals also call their village "Fenwick". Fen- referring to the wetlands and Wick- meaning"dwelling place," or "settlement"

Since they are nomadic and only have what the earth gives them. They are very resourceful. Masters of hunting, fishing, gathering, tracking. They are master craftsmen in things like pottery, animal domestication, farming and natural remedies.

("You know John cums wheat field? Rumor has John Cum spent years learning from the Veilwalkers on how to make his field's plentiful" "that may be true or not but all i know is after John Cum spent time with those strange characters he started providing enough wheat for the whole island")

Since they have no need for money or gold, they trade their fishing gear and such for promises and vows. Whether that be a promise to plant a flower for every fish you catch. Or a vow to be more virtuous. Legend is that those who keep these promises have abundance from whatever item they traded for.

There are many legends about this tribe. People are constantly telling different stories about who and what they are. But a few things are known

  - If you have an illness that seems fatal that has never been seen before, they can heal it
  - If you want to catch a fish? You use bait and lures that they make (legend has it that the fisherman who slayed the great tavern beast used fishing gear from the "Veilwalkers". As well as wood and fabrics to build his boat)
  - You want to travel through the thick forest? They will guide you through safely

Their true purpose is to tend to the earth. To till the soil, to trim the weeds, to instruct humans how to properly tend to the earth and animals (to those who seek it) and most importantly to protect it from the greedy.

If you're lucky enough to have one speak to you they simply tell you short lesson, things like "the earth has much to give to you if you'll let it"

### **The Pour Eternal (Shanty Town Folklore)**

*tattered, half-forgotten tavern tale, passed down through generations, scribbled by mad sailors and mistranslated by drunk historians*

*"Some gold buys breath. Some gold don't let the dead stay quiet."*

So it's said—though who said it first, no one rightly knows. Some say it was SackBeard, others claim it was carved into the ribs of the beast itself.

Long ago, afore the Windsunk sat in council, the First Captains buried pieces of a treasure too wild to keep and too wicked to share.

A cask. A Pour Eternal.

Brewed in madness, they say, by the founders of Aleforge and Sackbeard the Beast-Breaker himself. Brewed from blood pulled from the gullet of the slain Leviathan, stirred with spiritweed from Fenwick, and sealed with powdered gold so fine it whispered when poured.

They feared its power. Or maybe they feared each other with it. So the founders scattered the maps—six of 'em—each carved with riddles, songs, or half-words meant only for those who still dream in salt and storm.

Some maps are whispered to rest with the Mapmaker that vanished. Others tied to Hollowgrim's curses and the ghost riddles that slither through the cliffs.

But most say the words have been lost—mistranslated, burned, swallowed by the sea or by men who spoke too loud.

The tale shifts with the teller, but all hold to this:

When the Six come together—when the gold of the founders is weighed, named, and given—then the Pour Eternal shall rise from the sea or the soul.

Drink it, they say, and become the next god of pirates.

But speak the wrong name… and your kin will drown for all time.

Truth or tide-tale? Who knows.

But every map's got a truth in it. And truth, like grog, has a way of finding the ones thirsty enough to seek it.

### **Shanty Town's Political Factions**

**1. The Tide Callers:** A semi-religious order of mystics, navigators, and storm-readers. They believe the ocean is a divine force that rewards devotion and punishes arrogance. Most are former helmsmen, weather witches, or deep-sea interpreters.

**2. The Coral-eyed:** Once sailors and scavengers, these individuals spent far too long beneath the sea — trapped in wrecks, cursed by unknown gods, or "kissed by the trench." Now, they are something less than human and more than drowned.

**3. The Ashen Oath:** Hidden fanatics and idealists who believe the sea, pirates, and grog have tainted the world. They want to purge the Shoals, burn the beast bones, and restore "order through fire." They're the most dangerous when ignored.

**4. Pirate Captains and their crew:** The lifeblood of Shanty Town. These are the retired, rogue, or rising captains who either crashed here willingly or seek to reclaim their legend. Each crew is its own little nation, and they don't always get along.

**5. Drifters, Traders, and Travlers:** These are the non-permanent residents — merchants, adventurers, exiles, scholars, and outcasts. They move between Shanty Town, Roto, and beyond. Some are spies. Some are saints. Some are completely mad.

An excerpt from Jasper "no Eyes, all-Hands", resident of La-Di-Da Street, member of Windsunk Council concerning Providence:

*Scrawled with a hot nail on driftwood. Found nailed to a salt barrel in the Shanty Square.*

**The Truth of Providence (from Someone Who's Fought, Farted, and Forged in Shanty Town)**

Providence? Bah. Ye mean the gilded pisshole where cowards pickle their souls in tonic and call it "order." I seen it. Walked them clean cobbled lanes with their twitchy tonic-fiends starin' like barn owls on black powder. But ye want to know what we think of 'em? Good. Sit down, I'll talk. You'll learn something salty. We in Shanty Town remember Providence when it were just a driftwood sermon by some tonic-made monk screamin' on a rock. The same monk they call "Saint No-Snooze" now? Yeah, he once tried to drink in the Great Tavern and passed out tryin' to convert the urinal. We dragged him out by his robes and he shouted "Order\!" so many times we named a barstool after him. The Order still owes us four pints.

**Providence Breeds Tonic Slaves, We Raise Legends**

In Providence, they sip tonic like it's a holy elixir and pray to machines. In Shanty Town, we drink grog until we speak fluent storm, then we go steal what we want. They tithe to a spire. We toss our gold into a keg and drink 'til we forget we were ever poor.

**Politics? They Got Bells. We Got Fire.**

Providence rings bells. They pray five times a tide to some glowy clock-heart thing. They say their Cardinal Addy hears the gods. We say he just needs a stiff pour and a slap. Here in Shanty, we've got the Windsunk Council — old pirates who gave up sailing, not scheming. They decide who eats, who raids, and who gets tossed off the cliffs. Simple. Fair. Brutal.

**Trade With Providence? Only When We're Desperate or Bored**

Their fancy brewing tools come wrapped in lace. Ours are built from kraken bone, rusted chain, and spite. We don't want their clean wares or their prayers. We want their secrets. And trust me, one day some lucky crew's gonna crack that lake port like a crab leg and find out what really fuels those never-sleepin' bastards.

**They Call Us Sinners. We Call Ourselves Free.**

They pity us. Say we're lost souls adrift in a sea of drink and violence. But I've seen what their holy town does after dark — when the tonic kicks in and the masks slip. Their Friars cry, their Circs dance, and their merchants whisper to drowned things.

Truth is, Providence wears a crown of steel and bleeds the same as us. The only difference? We sing about it. They write sermons and hide the stains under prayer robes.

**So What's Shanty Town to Them?**

We're a threat. We're a memory they can't erase. We're the first pour of freedom after a life of kneeling. And we've got their sons, their daughters, and maybe their gods, all dancing to our drum.

They call us a den of swashbuckling sinners? Good. We call them next on the list.

*— Jasper "no Eyes, all-Hands", Windsunk Council Member, Burner of Bibles, First Piss of the Morning*

  - They pity us for our chaos, while dancing half-naked in honor of a liquor deity who may or may not exist.

If that ain't hypocrisy in a pint glass, I don't know what is.

Still… their craftsmanship's fine, their brewing tools work, and their young folk sometimes sneak off to become pirates. (Which we welcome, by the way — fresh meat always needed.)

**Political Balance**

Right now, it's a delicate seesaw.

Then the next Drunken Trials might be fought with cutlasses, not kegs.

Until then, we smile, we toast, and we wait.

Because pirates don't start wars — we end 'em.

*— Old Salty, barfly, spy, and scribe (never sober)*

#### ***On Roto:***

**Current Tensions**

Shanty Town and Roto haven't clashed — yet — but the air's thick with watchful silence. Roto's growing fast, and pirates don't trust fast-growing things unless they're cannonballs or rumors.

What started as a floating flea market has turned into a seaborne kingdom of coin, and that spooks the Windsunk Council. Not because they hate trade — gods know we trade — but because Roto runs without rules, yet makes enough coin to buy loyalty.

And lately? Some Shanty-born pirates have skipped the cliffs and gone straight to Roto, chasing easier profits and softer landings. That don't sit well with the Council.

There's talk that Roto's elite want to "clean up" some of the more colorful pirate factions around the outer reefs. If that means Shanty Town's "unregulated element," you can bet steel will follow.

**Perceptions & Prejudice**

To us? Roto folk are fence-lickers and coin-worshippers. Slick-tongued, soft-palmed merchants who believe a good deal's better than a good fight.

That said, not every pirate spits on Roto. Some respect their honest dishonesty — their flat merchant fees are cleaner than half the scams Shanty barkeeps run. But most of us think their taboo system is a joke — pretending something ain't allowed, but still selling it in the shadows? That's not law. That's cowardice in perfume.

**Political Balance**

There's a delicate, grizzled detente between Shanty Town and Roto. They don't regulate us, we don't raid them — because both sides know what happens if the keg tips over.

We bring muscle and mystique. They bring infrastructure and coin. Roto runs on markets, but markets don't float without pirate protection — and everyone remembers it was pirate loyalty that kept Xi's dream alive.

Still, Roto's line of merchant-lords, hand-picked heirs like Kim Jong Un, makes our Council twitch. Too clean. Too centralized. Too clever. If Roto ever decides piracy is bad for business?

Then we'll remind 'em who made business possible in the first place.

#### ***On Fenwick:***

**Current Tensions**

There ain't much blood between Shanty Town and Fenwick — mostly because we ain't stupid enough to piss off the woods. The Windsunk Council's unofficial stance is: "Let the moss monks keep their weird mud magic, and leave offerings when you pass by."

But trouble brews all the same. Some younger pirates, looking to score exotic gear or test their luck, plunder herbs, game, and spirit-marked stones from Fenwick lands. Those lads often don't come back. Or if they do, they're cursed — with rotting teeth, dry barrels, or dreams that scream.

There's whispers now that the Veilwalkers have marked a handful of pirates — not with blades, but with quiet watching. And when those watchers stop watching, bad things happen.

**Perceptions & Prejudice**

Most Shanty folk don't know what to make of the Veilwalkers.

Some call 'em garden ghosts, others call 'em mud-sucking mystics. The kinder ones call 'em healers, the crueler ones cursed monks with too much moss in their brains.

But even the drunkest salt knows this: you don't mock a Veilwalker to their face. You see one in the woods? You nod. You leave something behind. You speak plain.

They don't deal in coin, which rattles most pirates. They trade in promises — and if you break a promise to one? Your luck sinks like a cannonball in a leaky hull.

Still, tales fly fast:

  - That John Cum's miracle harvest came from sleeping in their garden.
  - That the Great Tavern Beast was caught with a Veilwalker's hook and spell-twined net.
  - That some pirates who vanished didn't die… they became one of them.

**Political Balance**

Fenwick don't play politics. But that makes 'em more powerful, not less.

They don't raid, don't negotiate, don't demand tribute. They just… exist. Rooted deep, like old stone under the sea.

That's what scares the Council — you can't predict what don't want anything.

Still, there's an unspoken accord:

  - We leave the woods alone.
  - We don't fish from cursed streams.
  - We give thanks to the land when it feeds us.

And in return? The Veilwalkers heal our wounded, guide our lost, and sometimes gift our boats with wind when all other winds fail.

But if we ever crossed their line? They wouldn't burn us with fire. They'd just let the earth take us back.

## **The Southern Bellows Nation**

South of the mainland, past waters few captains dare chart, lies the **Southern Bellows Nation (SBN)**, a reclusive, fog-wrapped confederation whose people have only just begun making contact with the Pintland Isles. For generations they kept the outside world at arm's length. Now, through backdoor channels and quiet deals, that isolation is beginning to crack.

The SBN is infamous for one thing above all: brewing the **worst IPAs known to exist** , bitter, rancid, and utterly beloved by their own people. Their entire culture, government, and worldview has grown up around the bottle, twisted into something both fascinating and faintly repulsive to outsiders.

  

### **The People**

  - **Appearance:** Bright, ivory-pale skin, almost luminous in low light — a trait locals attribute to generations spent indoors tending fermentation vats away from the sun. They dress in deep, dark amber apparel, said to mirror the color of their most treasured (and most vile) brews.
  - **Language:** They speak **Blowish**, a strange, rhythmic dialect built almost entirely around drawn-out "ooo" sounds ,  more chant than conversation, said to echo the low moan of wind through a bellows pipe.
  - **Values:** The SBN is a fiercely **nationalistic** and **self-sacrificial** society. Agnosticism is law,  no state religion, no worship, only the nation and the brew. Individual glory means little; what matters is what you give to the Bellows.

### **Government: The Brew-Right**

Much like Aleforge, power in the SBN isn't inherited, it's **earned through brewing and drinking**. The worse the IPA, the more respected the brewer. Rising through the ranks means surviving batches that would send lesser drinkers to a healer's bed.

At the top of this hierarchy sits **the Pale**, the highest-ranking official in the nation, tasked with:

  - Maintaining order across the Bellows regions
  - Overseeing the creation and quality control of IPAs nationwide
  - Serving as the closest thing the SBN has to a face for outsiders (though even most Pintlanders couldn't name the current one)

Titles beneath the Pale are rumored to include regional "Kegmasters" and traveling "Bittermen," though little is confirmed outside SBN borders.

### **Technology & Industry**

Despite their isolation, the SBN is surprisingly advanced:

  - **Ale Bean Processing:** Sophisticated machinery ,part mill, part alchemical still grinds and ferments their signature "Ale Beans" into the foul brews that fuel their economy.
  - **Aviation:** The SBN has mastered flight through **steam-powered gliders**, skeletal wing-craft that hiss and creak across the southern skies, letting them scout and travel without ever touching foreign soil.

This combination of chemical mastery and aerial engineering makes them far more capable than their reclusive reputation suggests, which is exactly what worries the people watching them.

### **Contact With the Outside World**

For unknown reasons, the SBN kept itself sealed off for generations. That's changing.

Recent activity has surfaced through:

  - **Illegal trade routes** smuggling Ale Beans and finished IPAs north
  - A quiet partnership with the **StormVeil Trading Company**, whose ships are the first foreign-flagged vessels the SBN has allowed near its coastline in living memory

This uptick hasn't gone unnoticed. Both the **Aleforge Special Drinkers Unit** and the **Providence Guard** have flagged the increased SBN presence as worth watching  but neither has been able to determine *why* the Bellows Nation is finally reaching out.

Their tithes—half of all earnings—flow like a golden river into the church's coffers, fueling a cryptic agenda whispered only in the shadows of Lagerhorn prayers, a mystery even to the sober outsider\!

These Apostles, bless their tireless souls, ne'er sleep, sustained by the sacred ClockHeart Tonic, their eyes agleam with divine fervor—and a touch of madness, perchance\! Every three hours, from Tidecall to Bloodwake, the grand Cardinal Addy's voice booms o'er the town, calling all to prayer with a bell forged from Roto's red-earth mineral, its clang a holy summons that sets the heart aflutter. 'Tis said the tonic's magic flows strongest during Grogsnap, when the Apostles' chants rise like a storm tide, weaving spells to ward off the chaos of Aleforge's unruly brews.

Our economy stands firm but stern, a fortress of faith where the church decrees what may be crafted or sold. Friars, lowly Apostles clad in robes stained with tonic spills, roam the cobbled streets on Beastwatch, judging the markets with a keen eye and a stricter hand. Sextons, the lowliest rank, haul tithes to the lake port in creaking carts, their greed oft outweighing their piety—curse their sticky fingers, pilfering an extra keg or two under Keeldrift's moon\! The Circs, masters of the brew, toil in hidden vats, their faces flushed from the tonic's heat, crafting each batch with chants to the unknown gods. The Kalifarts, rare as a sober night, dwell in lofty towers, scribbling variations of the tonic with quills dipped in fermented ink, seeking a brew to rival the golden Sun itself—though some whisper they chase a potion to raise the dead, a secret buried deeper than the ocean floor\!

The Cardinal, our exalted leader, commands from Patmos's spire, his voice a thunderclap on Bloodwake, his will the law of the land. At night, when the Hangover bell tolls, the townsfolk transform—merchants become poets, blacksmiths dance with wild abandon, and even the sternest Friar might weep for joy or rage. 'Tis the tonic's gift, they say, a dual nature bestowed by the unknown gods: by day, order; by night, a revelry that echoes the giant tavern's call. Yet, dark rumors swirl on Keeldrift winds—some claim the night's change hides a curse, that the tonic binds souls to Patmos's will, their personalities a mask for a deeper servitude.

Beyond the lake port, a fleet of stout ships sails to Roto Kaishi, their hulls laden with brewing gear, returning with red-earth mineral that glows like embers in the tonic's fire. The port itself is a marvel, built on stilts like Roto's kin, its docks alive with the slap of waves and the shouts of traders, guarded by Apostles who wield tonic-fueled strength. Once a year, on the Ascension of the Lord in Goldsun, Keg 4, the port hosts the Festival of the Red Tide, where the townsfolk offer a tithe of tonic to the sea, its crimson waves said to bless the next cycle's brew with divine potency.

**Providence Foreign Relations**

**Shanty Town: The Den of Swashbuckling Sinners**

To us in Providence, Shanty Town is a raucous pit of pirate revelry, its streets a maze of rum-soaked taverns and cutlass clashes, lit by the flicker of stolen lanterns. We trade little with them, for their goods—salty grog and tarnished trinkets—scarce tempt our markets, yet many a young Apostle hails from those dangerous lanes, fleeing the swashbuckler's life for Patmos's peace. Cardinal Addy, born to a Shanty whore, is our pride, his rise a testament to the unknown gods' mercy—though some jest he still carries a pirate's swagger on Bloodwake prayers\! The townsfolk eye Shanty folk with a mix of pity and awe, whispering that their rum flows like the tide, wild and untamed, unlike our refined whiskeys. Rumors swirl that their leaders plot to raid our lake port for tonic secrets, a notion that sends Friars to sharpen their staffs—yet we tolerate them, for their converts bolster our ranks.

**Roto Kaishi: The Stilted Trading Kin**

Roto Kaishi, perched on ocean stilts, is our chief partner, a bustling hub we view with grudging respect and wary eyes. Their ships bring silks, exotic woods, and spices that flavor our gins, while we export brewing tools and the rarest drops of ClockHeart Tonic—hoarded like gold, sipped only by the Cardinal's grace\! The townsfolk marvel at Roto's stacked houses, calling them a beggar's dream with an elite's crown, and trade tales of their spiced liquors over a glass of local bourbon. Yet, dark whispers haunt Keeldrift nights—Roto schemers, they say, toil in hidden vats to steal our tonic's recipe, their crimes and addictions a stain on their oriental splendor. The lake port buzzes with Roto sailors, their laughter a strange melody, and some Providence lasses flirt with the boldest, earning scowls from Sextons. We sail to them with ease, but trust is thin—Friars pray for their souls, while Kalifarts ponder if their spices hold a rival brew's secret\!

**Foreign Relations: The Strained Bond with Aleforge**

Oh, how the winds of Keeldrift carry tales of Aleforge, that unruly cradle of liquor and chaos, a place we in Providence regard with a mix of reverence and righteous scorn\! Our history with them is etched in the exile of the Monk Who Ne'er Slumber'd, a tale that sours our finest gin and fuels our prayers on Bloodwake. Let me recount the state of our bond, my quill wobbling with each sip of spiced rum\!

**A Legacy of Exile**

Hundreds of years past, in the dim mists of Stormtide, Keg 13, Bloodwake of the 236th year—or so the elders reckon—the Monk, our blessed founder, walked Aleforge's streets. There, amidst their taverns and brawling brewers, he crafted the sacred ClockHeart Tonic, a liquor so divine the unknown gods themselves whispered its recipe. But the Liquor Kings of Old Aleforge, those greedy souls drunk on power and common whiskey, deemed his concoction too holy for their unworthy hands. Fearing his growing influence—perchance his dabbling with the Cloister's dark mysteries or the amethyst crystals' power—they cast him out, banishing him across the sea to this rocky isle. On the 125th Tidecall of Keg 7, his vision birthed Providence, a haven of order to spite their chaos. The townsfolk here still curse Aleforge's name on Lagerhorn prayers, seeing their exile as the spark of our divine mission.

**Current Tensions**

Today, our relationship with Aleforge is a brittle truce, strained by trade and theology. They sprawl near the great Tavern, that hulking shell where pirates and traders revel yearly, a sight we view with pious disdain—such rowdy feasts mock our solemn Rites\! Aleforge trades in robust rums and bourbons, their markets loud with the clink of mugs, while we export the rare ClockHeart Tonic, a treasure guarded by the Cardinal and sipped only by the faithful elite. Our Friars haggle with their merchants, exchanging brewing tools for their exotic spices, yet tariffs rise like storm tides, each side seeking to choke the other's wealth. The townsfolk whisper that Aleforge envies our tonic, their Liquor Kings plotting to steal its secret—much like Roto's schemers—fueling a cold war of commerce.

**Perceptions and Prejudice**

To us, Aleforge is a den of excess, its people drunk on freedom and forgetful of the gods. We hear tales of their Tavern Beast slaying, a feat we grudgingly admire, but their annual feast—Stormtide, Keg 5, Lagerhorn—strikes us as pagan revelry, a contrast to our Feast of the Eternal Keg. Some say their leaders, descended from Sackbeard's crew, still bear pirate blood, their gin-fueled boasts a threat to our peace. Yet, a flicker of respect lingers—after all, it was Aleforge that birthed the Monk, and his spirit haunts their streets, they claim, guiding lost souls to our shores. Our Sextons eye their traders with suspicion, while the Kalifarts ponder if Aleforge's chaos hides a mystical key to the Cloister's beast.

**A Fragile Balance**

For now, we coexist, our lake port ships sailing past their Tavern on Grogsnap, our goods mingling but our hearts divided. The Cardinal preaches caution, fearing Aleforge's might, while the townsfolk dream of reclaiming the Monk's legacy—perchance his descendants will one day rule the Vats, as he foretold. Until then, we sip our whiskey and watch Aleforge's horizon, praying the unknown gods keep our tonic safe from their grasping hands.

**Foreign Relations: The Enigmatic Accord with Fenwick**

Beyond our rocky isle, the mists of Veilfrost, Keg 11, Keeldrift whisper tales of Fenwick, a wetland settlement shrouded in mystery and the earthy scent of its nomadic guardians, the Veilwalkers. To us in Providence, this place is a puzzle wrapped in nature's embrace, a realm we regard with cautious awe and a sip of smooth bourbon, for their ways defy our pious order. Let me unfurl this bond, my quill dancing with each grog-tinged thought\!

**A Timeless Presence**

The Veilwalkers, those ancient "earth-born" protectors birthed by woodland spirits—nymphs, gnomes, and fairies—precede even the Monk Who Ne'er Slumber'd's exile. Their history stretches back to the dawn of man, when barbarous "flesh-born" ravaged the land, prompting the spirits to mold these translucent guardians from soil and magic. Living tenfold the span of humans, they dwell in Fenwick, a cobbled hamlet named by islanders for its marshy Fen and humble Wick, moving only when the winds and waters decree—centuries to our fleeting years. Providence folk marvel at their longevity, whispering on Lagerhorn that they've seen the Tavern Beast's rise and fall, their ghostly forms a testament to the unknown gods' deeper will.

**A Relationship of Mutual Respect**

Our bond with Fenwick is one of quiet harmony, unmarred by the tariffs that sour our ties with Aleforge. The Veilwalkers, unbothered by our settlement, ask only that we honor the earth—a plea our Sextons heed by tithing seeds alongside coins, and our Friars bless with prayers on Tidecall. They trade not for gold but for vows—promises to plant flowers or live virtuously—and legend holds that John Cum's wheat fields bloomed after such a pact, feeding the island. We export our rare ClockHeart Tonic to them sparingly, a gesture of goodwill, receiving in return their masterful fishing gear and natural remedies, which our healers praise on Bloodwake. The townsfolk speak of their lures with reverence, some claiming Sackbeard's crew used Veilwalker crafts to slay the Tavern Beast—proof of their earth-given skill\!

**Perceptions Tinged with Unease**

To us, the Veilwalkers are mystics and healers, their ghostly transformations into "Veilwalkers" during spirit communions a sight that sets our hearts aflutter—yet we call them "dumb monks in the cave" with a tipsy chuckle, uneasy with their nameless ways. Their respect for nature aligns with our care for Patmos's gardens, but their lack of hierarchy irks our Cardinal, who sees no equal to his divine rule. Tales abound of their curses—famine and disease on those who despoil the earth—making our merchants tread lightly, lest a greedy Sexton's hand invite their wrath. Still, we admire their guidance through thick forests and their healing of strange ills, a boon to our pilgrims on Grogsnap journeys.

**A Delicate Balance**

Our relations thrive on this delicate dance—Fenwick's nomads tend the island's soul, while we tend its spirit. The Kalifarts ponder if their earth magic links to the Cloister's mysteries, perhaps guiding the Monk's vision, while the townsfolk dream of their wisdom enriching our tonic. For now, we coexist, our lake port ships passing their wetlands on Beastwatch, trading vows and wares, ever mindful of the earth's balance they guard. Yet, should we falter in respect, their haunting curse looms—a shadow even our whiskey can't dispel\!

### **Seamus Bonehardy (Nathan) "King of Kegs" — The Barrel Breakers / Ship: The Alemaster's Mortar**

I don't think they intended to leave that island, but once my mother became pregnant, my father is said to have become a completely different man and worked tirelessly to get us off the island. It ain't much right now, but as my own captain, I see a future full of gold and exploits unlike any of those known by the pirates I grew up around.

Seamus Bonehardy hired on as navigator to a treasure hunting outfit whose reputation rested entirely on enthusiasm, having in eleven prior expeditions recovered nothing at all. Its leader, Gilligan Rape, funded the enterprise on inherited money and showed no sign of running out of either funds or optimism. The twelfth expedition succeeded. Whether by Bonehardy's navigation or a better map broker, the party found a cave in country that should have been picked over generations earlier. The research assistant died to three separate traps on the way in. Beyond them lay a chamber holding a chest filled with gold. Gilligan's reaction to the sight made plain that his long and fruitless obsession had never been strictly financial. His enthusiasm was interrupted only by the recollection that Bonehardy was still in the room, at which point he drew a pistol and explained that the gold would be covering eleven expeditions' worth of losses and that Bonehardy would not be leaving to mention it.

Bonehardy's one advantage was position. He stood behind the open chest; Gilligan stood before it, in a state of some exposure. Bonehardy dropped and kicked the lid. The shot went wide. Indeed, on his 23rd birthday, he coordinated mutiny on *The Bellboy’s Boner* (his current ship of employment) and took the plunge into piracy. 

From that day onward, Sackbeard terrorized the waters of the eleven seas, slowly growing in ambition until one day finding that there were no ships left to pillage. After nearly a month of aimless roving, looking for *anyone* to pillage, Sackbeard was forced to take port to replenish supplies. It was here that he heard of the great shelled beast. It turns out that despite his prowess, the seas had not cleared for fear of him, but rather for terror of a monster risen from the depths which had sunk a dozen ships in a mere month. 

  

The arrival of this monster marked the start of a new famine. Crews left the seas in droves and could be paid no amount of money to return. Recently modernized nations had grown to rely too heavily on imports from their colonies and were now forced to ravage their own lands in search of enough resources to keep starvation at bay. Sackbeard meanwhile longed for a return to greatness- He spent years in isolation, slowly burning through his hidden stores of treasure and starving for even a taste of the adventures of times past. 

  

It was in these years that Sackbeard realized he was no man of the earth. No dry ground could match the feeling of sea-legs on a battle-hardened poop deck. No gentle breeze would dare rise to the overwhelming power of the four winds battling for control of the sails. No tavern song held a candle to the rumble of a belowdecks shanty. Sackbeard had no purpose left if not to kill this monster. 

  

Shortly after this realization, Sackbeard began a series of daring island hopping missions and the tavern whispers began to say that the pirate king was again on the move. On the last day of Goldsun in the year 178, Sackbeard paid out the last of his gold to eleven captains in perhaps the largest pirate council ever before devised. Each captain in turn swore to join the ranks of Sackbeard’s armada and fight the foul beast that had invaded their beloved seas. 

  

The next day, the eleven gathered crews struck out to find and kill the dreaded monster. After three days of sailing, a strange bout of winds took the armada off of regular trade paths. However, this turned out to be in their favor, as the waters soon grew disturbed at the presence of the great beast. At once, each captain enacted the orders given by Sackbeard, fanning out to prepare for a cannon barrage. However, none of the crews had ever shot cannon at anything moving so quickly in the water and plans quickly became fuel for chaos, as captains tried to decide between holding formation and retreating. 

  

Four of the eleven ships were lost in this chaos, as the beast ripped indiscriminately into their hulls. Indeed, Sackbeard’s ship would have been lost if not for a stroke of inspiration. His mast had fallen perpendicular to its prior position, laying on the ship useless for all but one purpose. As the beast came round for another series of attacks, Sackbeard dropped anchor and ordered all men aboard belowdecks that they might turn the ship. Terrified were the men of the belowdecks, but more terrified were they of Sackbeard in the heat of battle. All men went below and turned the ship until the mast pointed at the monster like a lance. It charged ravenously, aiming to take off the ship’s front in one bite. It was betrayed by its power, as the anchor held fast and drove the mast further and further into the skull of the charging beast. 

  

The beast had been slain. Now remained the task of survival for those that remained- Most ships were taking on water and rushed for the nearest island. Sackbeard followed, but only after dispatching orders that any ship still standing should attempt to fasten the great beast and his attached ship to their tow holds and bring them to shore. The undertaking was massive and would require the assistance of two more crews, but all eventually agreed that the beast would be cut from its shell and sunk along with Sackbeard’s ship. Fortune now smiled on the pirates, as their previous loss of course had resulted in them finding an island untouched by the colonial empires. As such, the shell would be dragged ashore and used as the roof for The Great Shell Tavern and it would serve as the place of pirate celebration for many cycles to come.

# **IV. WORLD HISTORY & TIMELINE**

**Current Date: Cycle 94, Year 466**

## **The Deep Past: Lagendale & the Rotted Soul**

Lagendale - the historic kingdom which ruled these lands thousands of years ago. The kingdom became solidified after a barbarian attack threatened the sovereignty of what is now the Pintland Isles. The first King of Lagendale gathered all the simple farmers of the land together to form a small militia and fight against the barbarian horde. This was the beginning of a great empire which ruled many of the eleven seas for hundreds of years. The furthest extent of the empire reached from what is now West Pint to the wild lands of the far west. Lagendale never was able to fully settle to the east of the Breakback Mountains due to many factors, but mainly because of the untamed jungle we still see today and the Lagenites religious superstitions regarding the Veilwalkers (what is now Fenwick used to be a much larger encampment of Veilwalkers).

### **The Rotted Soul**

The History of Horrors in Lagendale:

  

For centuries, the Kingdom of Lagendale ruled the Westpint region of the Pintland Isles, a quiet agricultural power, governed not by kings but by appointed councils drawn from villages across the land. It was a measured, orderly civilization. And for a long time, it was safe. In those ancient days, the boundary between the mortal world and the immortal Veil was carefully maintained. The Veilwalkers, ageless, magical beings bound to the living earth, rarely crossed into human affairs, and humans were wise enough not to seek them out. That peace ended with a farmer.

  

The Forbidden Union

  

During the reign of the 33rd Council, a farmer from the northern fields wandered into the Foreverglades, an ancient forest shared by humans and Veilwalkers in their respective seasons, and encountered something he was never meant to find: a female Veilwalker. Female Walkers are seldom seen. By Veilwalker law, they are barred from serving as Elders or ambassadors, keeping them largely out of contact with the outside world. This particular Walker had already taken The Vow of Mate, a sacred and unbreakable bond in Veil culture. When two Walkers take this vow before the Elders, they are bound to one another for eternity, obligated to produce offspring and strengthen the magic of the next generation. Breaking the vow carries extreme punishment. None of that stopped the farmer. He returned to the Foreverglades again and again. And in the 8th year of the 33rd Council's reign, something previously unimaginable occurred: a child was conceived, half human, half Veilwalker. A halfbreed. The first of its kind.

  

The First Treaty

  

When the union was discovered, it triggered a crisis on both sides. The Veilwalker Elders and the Lagendale Council entered into a tense negotiation, ultimately producing the first-ever Walker-Human treaty: 

  

The humans would raise the halfbreed child on behalf of the Walkers, sparing the Veil from the burden of an unprecedented situation while avoiding the forbidden act of spilling Walker blood. In return, the Veil would bless Lagendale's fields indefinitely, so long as a human with Walker blood remained in that family line. Both powers walked away satisfied. There was, however, one party no one bothered to consult: the male Veilwalker whose mate had broken their Vow.

  

The Wronged Walker

  

He brought his grievance before the Elders with full justification. He had been forsaken, the only Walker in recorded history abandoned by a bonded mate. He demanded restitution. The Elders had no precedent. The offense crossed racial lines, fell outside established law, and made everyone uncomfortable. So they dismissed him. It was the worst decision they ever made.

  

The Rotten King

  

The Rotted Soul was a king in between the 2nd and 3rd dynasties of Lagendale. As far as history knows, he was not a part of either royal family. He was a King so evil and tyrannical that his own people wiped his true name from history. The Rotted Soul rose to power through shadowy means, and eventually declared himself as the god-king of Lagendale after killing all of 138 Council members at once. It is said that their flesh became as dead men even as they stood upon the council floor.

  

In the 9th year of the 33rd Council's reign, a volcanic earthquake obliterated the island of Vodkonia. It was no natural disaster. The Wronged Walker, now twisted by grief and rage, had done what no Veilwalker had done before: he drew magic from the earth not to nurture it, but to destroy it. This act violated the most sacred law of the Veil. It also transformed him into something else entirely.

  

What followed was a masterpiece of calculated cruelty.

  

Disguising himself as a warrior of light, the Wronged Walker manipulated the Lagendale Council into believing that the Vodkonian refugees, innocent people fleeing for their lives, were invaders intent on conquest. He led the slaughter personally. He was celebrated as a hero. At the height of his manufactured glory, he assassinated every member of the 33rd Council and crowned himself High King of Lagendale.

  

For one year, he ruled fairly. He was searching for the farmer and for the halfbreed child. When he realized the people were hiding them, his patience ran out. He began using dark magic to melt the flesh from anyone who defied him. The hero became a monster in plain sight. With no one left to challenge his rule, he became a tumor on a once great nation. Friends and foe alike were left to rot and his wrath knew no end. He wore clothes made from the skin of his enemies and would light the streets at night by burning slaves alive. His kind had a name for what he had become: a VeilRunner.

  

**What Is a VeilRunner?**

  

A VeilRunner is a Veilwalker who has turned against the very earth they were bound to protect. Where normal Walkers channel magic as a force of growth and life, a VeilRunner's power manifests as rot and combative decay, a corrupting force capable of unraveling all living things if left unchecked. The Elders, slow to act and now deeply alarmed, dispatched multiple envoys to stop him. All failed. The Rotten King had spent years mastering destructive magic that his kin had never developed defenses against. He was, in the most literal sense, something new. Some reports even say that the very ground under his step would die as he walked through the empire. Many believe this to be an exaggerated account, but the great desert to the south of modern Pintland is believed to be the site where he was eventually overcome and murdered, killing the whole land with his last breath.

  

**The River Coffin**

  

Out of desperation, the Elders and the Lagendale militia devised a joint solution using one of the most powerful and costly rituals in existence: The River Coffin. He was buried not in a tomb or a grave, but in a prison, never to be found again.

  

**Step 1**, Isolation: By methods that remain undocumented, the Rotten King was captured and sealed inside a coffin of obsidian crystal, bound with ancient tomes. Cut off from the earth, his power was neutralized.

  

**Step 2,** Burial: The coffin was lowered into a deep underground grave, placing it as far from any living source of power as possible. He is said to be buried in a place so deep and so dark that no man will ever find his remains, nor should they want to, for a dark magic was placed upon his sepulcher which is said to bring rot to your nation, your family and your own soul.

  

**Step 3,** Soul Scrambling: A VeilRunner's soul carries the same rot as its body. Left uncontained, it can seep free of the corpse and cause psychological destruction across the land. To prevent this, the burial site was surrounded by labyrinthine passages, winding, river-like corridors designed to trap and confuse the soul so it could never find its way to the surface.

  

**Step 4,** Fertilization: The sealing magic requires living sacrifice to sustain itself. Blood must be spilled to bind the flesh and soul together permanently.

  

What little information survives about his burial place is just as horrific. Many layers deep, the labyrinth is built underneath already existing catacombs. A stone labyrinth, of unbelievable complexity and so unparalleled that it consumes all who enter. Designed as a prison, he was laid there, never to be found again. The labyrinth is not a simple maze, it is a series of trials designed to break the soul of those who enter… or of those who try to escape.

  

**The Betrayal Beneath the Earth**

  

In the final year of the Rotten King's reign, a volunteer force of workers and craftsmen descended underground and spent over 400 days constructing the largest labyrinth in human history. When the work was done and the King was sealed, the militia that had protected those workers throughout the entire project turned on them, executing over 800 men to complete the sacrifice. The militia leadership intended to erase this history entirely. They killed the soldiers who carried out the massacre to ensure no witnesses survived. All the slaves who buried his body were killed, and all the slaves who killed them were slaughtered as well, and thus, the Rotted Soul was lost to time.

  

One escaped.

  

The Guardian was the last hope of the Lagendale people, in the event that the Rotted Soul returned from the afterlife, they needed him to be contained in the endless horror they had devised. The many buried souls were believed to offer protection in keeping the Rotted Soul contained within the prison tomb. One unfortunate soul, an unnamed slave, was chosen to be the guardian of the prison tomb. All the mages and mystics gathered together to cover this sacrificial man with the most powerful of protection spells and he was sent into the tomb and sealed off along with it. It's unknown if his body remains, but his spirit is there guarding the tomb and ready to hold back the Rotted Soul should he ever return.

  

What Remains

  

No one knows with certainty what became of Lagendale. Some say the families of the slaughtered workers rose up and tore the kingdom apart from within. Others say foreign invaders arrived and scattered what remained of the people across the Isles.

  

What is known is this:

  

The blood of the halfbreed still flows somewhere in the Pintland Isles.

  

The lost labyrinth of Lagendale still stands beneath the earth, carved with the echoes of 800 betrayed men.

  

And the Rotten King is still down there, sealed, starved, and waiting.

  

If a VeilRunner ever reaches the surface again, the world above will not survive it.

  

There are many dangers in the labyrinth, some attacking your body, some attacking your brain, but the most dangerous are those which attack your soul. Should any adventurer be foolish enough to enter the tomb, they should be strong in body, steadfast in mind and pure of soul, lest they join the dead and leave their soul to Rot with Him.

  

The labyrinth lies beneath Hoegaarden, deep below the catacombs, sealed for millennia. Recent events in Pintland — the slaughter of the Cloister Beast, the attack on Aleforge from Sackbeard's cursed crew — have disturbed things best left untouched, and Jack Anqoak believes the dark magic keeping the Guardian's spirit alive may have begun to unravel.

## **The Rise of the VeilRunner**

The Liquor Kings were successful at defeating the Rotted Soul, the infamous VeilRunner responsible for the fall of Lagendale. But upon his defeat, the soul of the King was released into the Pintland Isles and beyond; threatening to wreak havoc in the world. While only one VeilRunner is currently known, the circumstances that lead to a Veilwalker becoming a Runner is known and replicable, meaning the spirit of the Rotted soul is fully capable of creating more walker incels by disrupting the mating process and manipulating humans into disrupting the mating ritual of the ancient people. No one knows what the soul of the Runner is doing in the world right now and what affect he will have on national security and foreign relations.

  
  

## **The Age of the Veilwalkers & the Great Beasts**

Long before the founding of the towns, the Veilwalkers tended the Isles. The great beast skeletons that now serve as landmarks were living creatures in some earlier age. The Tavern Beast itself was one such creature. Whether these beasts were related, whether the scattered crystals and shrine anomalies all connect to something larger, and whether the animal skeletons and the Cloister's dimensional anomalies share a common origin are questions that scholars like Ignatius De Montfort and Reverend Ambrose Ravenscroft have dedicated themselves to answering.

## **The Shrines**

The ancient shrines scattered across the Isles were most likely built by the same order of monks who later constructed the Cloister, though the shrines predate the Cloister by a considerable margin. At some later point, a completely separate group used the shrines to hide valuable pieces of armor — four pieces per set, six sets total. This group has since dispersed or died. Their motivations remain unknown. Nobody knows what happens when a full set is assembled.

## **Sackbeard's Conquest & the Founding of the Tavern**

A few hundred years ago, the great beast now enshrined in the Tavern awoke and began its campaign of destruction. Sackbeard assembled an armada, killed the beast, and oversaw the construction of the Tavern in its hollowed shell. The annual pirate feast that followed became the nucleus around which Shanty Town grew, and Sackbeard himself became the most legendary pirate name in the eleven seas.

What the celebrations concealed is this: prior to his death, Sackbeard had attacked a Veilwalker settlement seeking to steal their nature-secrets. His first mate Gideon Drake, part Veilwalker himself, turned on him, killed him in a duel, cursed the crew with eternal life, and banished them to the Nether.

## **The Discovery of the Cloister Phenomenon & the Founding of Shanty Town**

As the annual Tavern feast grew larger, the Cloister became a point of curiosity for the growing population on the cliffs. Shanty Town formed organically from the accumulation of retired pirates crashing their ships and swearing loyalty to the Shoals. The Windsunk Council codified the first laws.

## **The Founding of Providence**

The Monk Who Ne'er Slumber'd was exiled from Aleforge, sailed across the sea, and founded Providence on divine instruction. The ClockHeart Tonic became both the economy and the religion of the new city.

## **Xi Jin Ping Founds Roto Kaiishi**

Xi discovered the pirate armada during a string of terrible luck, saw an opportunity, and built the world's first ocean-bound free market city. Roto grew from a floating shop into a seaborne nation-state governed by a chain of personally selected merchant-philosophers.

## **The Cloudrunners Built / Aleforge Founded**

The two surviving Cloudrunners were constructed in an era that has since been forgotten. Aleforge was founded on a small island nearby to the Shelled Tavern, its brewing culture developing naturally from the ideal terrain. The Gilded Tankard became the center of civic and spiritual life.

## **The Founding of Owe Block**

Owe Block was founded out of the social and religious disparities generated by Providence's rigid hierarchy. It became a district city operating on its own terms, with its own factions, economy, and underground markets.

## **The New Cloister Built / MAA Founded**

The current Cloister structure replaced an older one and was inhabited first by the monk order and then, after their disappearance, by the Prophet. The MAA was founded around this period, opposing the culture of drinking that the Drunken Trials perpetuated.

## **Fentmaxxer Builds the Observatory**

35 years before the Cloister Beast storyline, Fentmaxxer hastily constructed his observatory on Killdeath Peak around what he believed was a meteor impact site. Inside the egg of the Warden, he found his obsession.

## **Concept III: The Liquor King Trials**

A new villager has prepared a special event to honor the 20th anniversary of the MAA's glorious defeat\!

While the killing of the MAA was celebrated by almost everyone in Aleforge, there was one person, or rather one child, who mourned their loss. Growing up with no parents, Maximilian was guided by nothing but confusion and rage. Maximilian knew that even with unlimited knowledge, the human lifespan was far too short for the years of innovations he would need in order to build the perfect hell for the liquor kings. Maximilian used a combination of crytotech, social climbing, and in some periods, downright slavery in order to maximize the technological progression during his "waking years." There were risks to this strategy- The slaves could revolt, for instance. Maximilian, however, was a master at setting people up against eachother and designing tight enough security systems that such revolts never worked. At the very least, all personnel were killed and he had to wake up to find a new workforce.

This strategy worked for centuries- Maximilian was able to direct the construction of a space station and many advanced systems. The perfect bed on which he could build a new law and, more importantly, his own hell in which to place the liquor kings.

That said, the systems weren't perfect. One year, the entire workforce made it to Maximilian's cryo-chamber before being massacred by the automated security force. For a few days he survived alone in the station, but his need for guidance and protection eventually led him to the liquor kings' cells.

He had only ever heard horror stories about the lives that the kings had lived. Maximilian told his crew that these men were the worst sort of criminal ever to roam the isles- alcoholics, murderers, and worse, homosexuals. However, Dimtri knew that he would die without the help of an adult. For that reason, he awoke the liquor kings from their sleep and receded to the air vents to watch their conduct and decide if they could be trusted.

When the kings awoke, they had all been separated. Surrounding them was technology from centuries after their lives were supposed to have happened. They stumbled around, understandably baffled, for hours upon hours before eventually meeting up and putting together a plan to get to Maximilian and end his century-spanning masochism. During this time, Jagerbauhm befriended Dmitri, Jamison Pilsner died an abundance of deaths, and Jack Anqok actually did a great job solving some puzzles.

Finally, the kings managed to kill Maximilian, escape his space station, and return to the Pintland Isles. But what awaited them there? Had the world changed more than they would be able to fathom or had their little corner of heaven remained as they remembered it?

# **VI. CULTURE, RELIGION & BUSINESS**

## **The Drunken Trials (Cultural Event)**

The Drunken Trials are the most important annual event in Aleforge and arguably the most famous cultural event in the Pintland Isles. Every year, competitors from across the region gather to drink magical Aleforge ale and compete in a series of trials, games, and challenges that test wit, strength, and endurance. The winner is crowned Liquor King for the year and their name is etched into the walls of the Gilded Tankard.

The ale served during the Trials is brewed by Glub Tuppus Wepple from the Gilded Tankard's magical spring — it cannot cause hangovers or alcohol poisoning but carries potent mystical effects the more one drinks. Competitors track Ale Points throughout the event, usable to gain advantages or force others to drink.

## **The Gilded Tankard (Business)**

The primary inn and brewing facility of Aleforge. Owned and operated by Glub Tuppus Wepple. Home to the annual Drunken Trials, the Gilded Tankard Grant (government-funded facilities upgrade under Drake's mayoralty), and Gregor Ashford's bard performances. Currently, the Tankard's entire ale stockpile is under Goldcoral Inc. control after Jack seized it from Wepple.

## **Goldcoral Incorporated (Business)**

Goldcoral Inc is a merchant business specializing in "aggressive mercantilism." They bring a lot of profits in but not much transparency on where those profits come from. As a result, much of the company's trade is through Roto, where low regulations and high profit margins reign supreme. They are headquartered in Providence, however all their trade and most importantly, original books are through Roto. The wealthy investors in Providence only see what they want to see, profits.

They are the owners of the ship The Bastards Requiem and the employers of the Foamrot Scallywag crew. This is a new company, currently privately owned by the Liquor King Jack. After Jacks performance in the Last Pour he founded the company and placed the ship under it's ownership. He is currently in search of a worthy crew to start bringing in higher profits for himself and future investors.

  

The company was involved in a scandal after investing a large sum of money into the Gilded Tankard, Aleforge's finest brewery. The owner, Glub Tuppus Wepple then gambled that investment money and unfortunately lost all of it. Upon hearing this news, Anqoak severely beat Glub Tuppus Wepple and stole the entire supply of Wepple Ale, giving to his company as new inventory. Currently, this inventory is being sold in secret at Jack Anqoak's Grog Galley

  

## **The Church of Patmos (Religious)**

Providence's governing religion, founded by the Monk Who Ne'er Slumber'd. All major trade, commerce, and cultural activity in Providence flows through Patmos's hierarchy. Half the population serves as Apostles. Tithes flow upward at fifty percent of all earnings. The church's agenda is partially public (order, sobriety, devotion) and partially whispered (certain scholars believe Patmos binds souls to its will through the tonic). The Cardinal — currently Addy — commands from Patmos's spire.

## **Veilwalker Trade & Custom (Cultural/Religious)**

At the birth of man a tribe of barbarians pillaged across the earth destroying all semblance of life and nature. The woodland spirits (nymphs, gnomes, fairies and such) could do little against them. So they used their combined magic to birth "protectors" from the earth. The protectors are what's known as the "veilwalkers". They were born from the dirt and soil of the earth. They refer to humans as "The flesh born" and themselves as "earth born". They live simple lives and live off of only what the earth provides for them. Hence they're architecture being cobbled together. They believe in a great respect for nature and the earth. They believe that nature should not conquer man nor that man should conquer nature but that they live in harmony.

## **The Aleforge Bond Market (Financial)**

Instituted by Gideon Drake and later expanded by the Liquor Kings during the Stewards of Aleforge crisis. Aleforge bonds are interest-bearing instruments sold to individuals and neighboring towns to raise capital. Roto traders were enthusiastic buyers. The Kings issued multiple rounds of bonds during the crisis, at increasingly unfavorable interest rates. The resulting debt was handed to Seamus Bonehardy's mayoral administration.

## **Aldridge Industries (Business)**

Named for the Aldridge family — of which Maximilian Andrew Aldridge was the last known member. The current state and operations of this business are not fully documented.

  
  

## **Providence Educational Foundation for Historical Preservation / Aleforge Restoration Association (Cultural)**

Two of the civic institutions operating in their respective cities. The Providence foundation maintains historical records and physical structures. The Aleforge Restoration Association has been involved in repairs to the Cloister over the years.

# **X. THE PINTLAND CALENDAR**

The Pintland calendar is organized as follows:

## **Units of Time**

  - Cycle: A 5-year period.
  - Year: A 360-day period.
  - Season (Month): A 117-day period. Three seasons per year.
  - Keg (Week): A 9-day period — just enough time for a proper bender.
  - Holiday Keg: Because 3 seasons x 13 kegs = 351 days, there are 9 leftover days at the end of each year. These form a holiday Keg at the end of each year, from day 351 to 360. This is the time of year when the pirates gather at Sackbeards tavern

## **The Three Seasons**

  - Stormtide — The wet season. Dangerous seas. 117 days.
  - Goldsun — The dry, sunny season. Calm seas. 117days.
  - Veilfrost — 117 days of night. The Veilwalkers are most active during this season. Mystical anomalies are most frequent. Things best left alone tend to stir.

## **The Nine Days of the Keg**

  - Mooringday
  - Brewsday
  - Tidecall
  - Grogsnap
